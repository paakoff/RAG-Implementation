---
title: Coping with Sadness and Low Mood
topic: sadness
---
Sadness is a natural response to loss, disappointment and loneliness. It often signals that something matters to us. Allowing sadness, rather than fighting it, can help it move through.

Gentle actions for low days. Get some daylight, ideally in the morning. Take a short walk. Eat something nourishing. Shower and change clothes. Send a message to one person. Small acts of care add up.

Loneliness. Loneliness is common, especially during transitions such as starting college or moving. Try low-pressure connection: join a club around an interest, study in a shared space, volunteer, or reconnect with an old friend with a simple message.

Grief. Grief has no fixed timeline and comes in waves. It can include sadness, anger, guilt, numbness and even relief. Rituals, writing to the person you lost, and talking with others who knew them can help.

When to seek help. If low mood, loss of interest, hopelessness, changes in sleep or appetite, or trouble functioning last most days for two weeks or more, it may be depression, which is very treatable. Reach out to a counselor or healthcare provider. If you have thoughts of suicide, call or text 988 in the US or contact local emergency services right away.
