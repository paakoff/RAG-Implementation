---
title: Breathing and Relaxation Exercises
topic: anxiety
---
Slow breathing activates the parasympathetic nervous system, the body's "rest and digest" mode, and can reduce feelings of panic and stress within a few minutes.

Box breathing. Breathe in through your nose for a count of 4, hold for 4, breathe out for 4, hold for 4. Repeat for four to six rounds. Imagine tracing the sides of a square.

4-7-8 breathing. Breathe in quietly through the nose for 4 counts, hold for 7 counts, and exhale slowly through the mouth for 8 counts. Start with four cycles. A long exhale is the most calming part.

Physiological sigh. Take a full breath in through the nose, then a second short sip of air on top of it, then a long, slow exhale through the mouth. One to three sighs can quickly lower acute stress.

Progressive muscle relaxation. Starting at your feet, tense a muscle group for about five seconds, then release for ten seconds and notice the difference. Move up through calves, thighs, stomach, hands, arms, shoulders and face. This is helpful before sleep.

If breathing exercises make you dizzy, breathe more gently and shorten the counts. Stop if you feel unwell.
