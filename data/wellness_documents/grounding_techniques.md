---
title: Grounding Techniques
topic: anxiety
---
Grounding techniques bring attention back to the present moment when you feel overwhelmed, panicky, numb, or caught in a loop of distressing thoughts or memories.

The 5-4-3-2-1 technique. Name 5 things you can see, 4 things you can touch, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste. Say them out loud or in your head slowly.

Physical grounding. Press your feet firmly into the floor and notice the pressure. Hold something cold, like an ice cube or a cold drink. Run your hands under cool water. Notice the texture of your clothing or a chair.

Mental grounding. Describe your surroundings in detail as if to someone on the phone. Count backwards from 100 by sevens. Name animals or cities alphabetically.

Soothing grounding. Say a kind statement to yourself, such as "This feeling is intense, but it will pass. I am safe right now." Picture a place where you feel calm, using all five senses.

Grounding does not make problems disappear, but it can lower the intensity of emotion enough for you to think clearly and choose your next step.
