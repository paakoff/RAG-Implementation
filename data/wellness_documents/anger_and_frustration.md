---
title: Working with Anger and Frustration
topic: anger
---
Anger is a valid emotion that often signals a boundary has been crossed, a need is unmet, or something feels unfair. The goal is not to get rid of anger but to express it in ways that don't harm you or others.

Pause before reacting. Notice early signs such as a hot face, clenched jaw or fast heartbeat. Step away for 10 to 20 minutes, breathe slowly, or move your body to discharge energy before responding.

Find the need underneath. Ask: What am I really upset about? Is there hurt, fear or disappointment underneath the anger? What do I need right now - respect, rest, fairness, to be heard?

Assertive communication. Use "I" statements: "I feel frustrated when plans change last minute, because I've organized my day around them. Could we agree on more notice next time?" Assertiveness respects both your needs and the other person.

Release safely. Physical exercise, writing an unsent letter, or talking it through with someone you trust can help process anger. If anger often leads to aggression, broken relationships or thoughts of hurting someone, talk to a professional.
