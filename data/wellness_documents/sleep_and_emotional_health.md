---
title: Sleep and Emotional Health
topic: sleep
---
Sleep and mood are closely linked. Poor sleep makes emotions feel more intense and harder to manage, and stress and anxiety make sleep harder. Most adults need 7 to 9 hours.

Sleep hygiene basics. Keep a consistent wake-up time, even on weekends. Get morning light. Limit caffeine after early afternoon. Keep the bedroom cool, dark and quiet. Avoid screens for 30 to 60 minutes before bed or use night mode.

Wind-down routine. Spend the last 30 minutes before bed doing something calming, such as reading, stretching, a warm shower, or progressive muscle relaxation.

If you can't sleep. If you're awake for more than about 20 minutes, get up and do something quiet in dim light until you feel sleepy, then return to bed. This keeps the bed associated with sleep, not frustration.

Racing thoughts at night. Keep a notebook by the bed. Write down worries and tomorrow's to-do list so your mind can let go of them. Try the 4-7-8 breathing exercise.

Ongoing insomnia that lasts weeks deserves a conversation with a healthcare provider. CBT for insomnia (CBT-I) is an effective, non-medication treatment.
