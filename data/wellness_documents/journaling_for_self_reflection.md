---
title: Journaling for Self-Reflection
topic: reflection
---
Expressive writing about thoughts and feelings has been linked with reduced stress and improved mood for many people. Journaling helps you notice patterns, process experiences and understand what you need.

How to start. Write for 10 to 15 minutes without worrying about grammar. Write about what happened, how you felt, and what it meant to you. Nobody else needs to read it.

Reflection prompts. What emotion was strongest today, and where did I feel it in my body? What drained my energy and what restored it? What am I avoiding, and why? What is one thing I handled well this week? What would I like to let go of?

Gratitude journaling. Each evening, write three specific things that went well and why they happened. Specific details ("my roommate made me tea when I was stressed") work better than general ones.

Mood tracking. Rating your mood from 1 to 10 each day and noting sleep, exercise, social contact and stressors can reveal patterns, such as low mood after poor sleep. Look for trends over weeks rather than judging a single day.

Values reflection. Write about what matters most to you, such as family, learning, creativity or kindness. When you feel lost, ask what small action today would move you toward one of those values.
